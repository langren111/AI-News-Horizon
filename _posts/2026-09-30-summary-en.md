## [CounterSteer: Activation Steering Defense Against Indirect Prompt Injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36570) ⭐️ 9.0/10

CounterSteer introduces an inference-time defense that fits a residual-stream direction per model from paired episodes and subtracts it from every tool-result token during prefill, requiring no fine-tuning, auxiliary model, or added tokens. Across five open-weights models (8B–106B), held-out attack success drops from 0.21–1.00 undefended to 0.00–0.17 defended, and AgentDojo compromise rate falls from 0.10–0.49 to 0.006–0.079 at 93–100% benign utility. Indirect prompt injection is a critical security vulnerability for LLM agents that ingest untrusted retrieved text, and CounterSteer offers a lightweight, always-on defense that avoids the utility losses or weight modifications seen in comparable defenses. Its strong empirical results across multiple vendor lineages make it highly relevant to AI safety, agent security, and practical deployment. The defense requires white-box serving and tool-result span boundaries, and while it largely neutralizes instructional takeover, parameter manipulation (attacker-chosen arguments in legitimate calls) is only partially resisted at 13 of 18 cases. White-box gradient attacks through the deployed vector compromise at most 2 of 52 episodes, and none of 2,052 replayed human red-team attacks succeeds, though a black-box framing search cracks 3 of 18 development samples.

rss · ArXiv CS.AI · Sep 30, 04:00

**Background**: Indirect prompt injection occurs when an LLM agent treats untrusted retrieved text, such as web content or tool outputs, as instructions, potentially leading to unauthorized actions. Activation steering is a technique that modifies a model's internal residual-stream representations at inference time to steer behavior without changing weights. AgentDojo is a widely used benchmark for evaluating prompt injection attacks and defenses in LLM agents.

## [Study Finds Cognitive Diversity Does Not Drive Multi-Agent Debate Gains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35875) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A large-scale study across 23 small open-weight language models from eleven vendor families, five tasks, and over 5,500 debate and control runs tested whether cognitive diversity among agents drives multi-agent debate (MAD) gains, and rejected the hypothesis on all three diversity axes: personas, sampling temperature, and model identity. At matched generation budgets, debate tied or lost to self-consistency sampling while costing 1.6x wall-clock time and 3.4x tokens. This negative result challenges a core assumption behind multi-agent debate research and suggests that reported MAD gains may largely be an ensemble-sampling effect rather than a product of genuine cognitive diversity. It provides a budget-matched, contamination-checked baseline that future debate mechanisms should be required to clear, directly informing work on LLM agents and reasoning. Persona prompting reduced accuracy, and a dose-response experiment over each model's full combinatorial persona space showed the cost is a 'persona tax' rather than a diversity tax, with redundant personas hurting most while maximally diverse teams recovered part of the loss. Mixed-model teams lost to majority votes over their own rosters, accuracy tracked member capability rather than heterogeneity, nearly all benefit came from the first exchange of answers, and a pervasive measurement hazard was identified where debate transcripts silently overflow serving context windows, whose correction alone moved the debate-versus-sampling comparison from -1.8 points to parity.

rss · ArXiv CS.AI · Sep 30, 04:00

**Background**: Multi-agent debate (MAD) is a technique in which multiple LLM instances exchange answers and critiques over several rounds to improve reasoning and factuality over single-model inference, and prior work typically treats the agents as symmetric peers. Small language models (SLMs) are compact models, typically under forty billion parameters, that can be trained or hosted on consumer hardware and often retain benchmark headroom on reasoning tasks. Self-consistency sampling is a simpler baseline that runs the same prompt multiple times at non-zero temperature and takes the majority answer, and this paper compares debate against it under matched generation budgets.

## [REST: A Representation-Supervised Objective for Latent Recursive LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36159) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new arXiv paper (2609.36159) identifies four failures of cross-entropy-only training in latent recursive LLM systems and proposes REST (REpresentation-Supervised Thoughts), a training objective that converts four properties of a valid thought representation—causality, minimality, separability, and stability—into differentiable losses added to CE. Across 7 benchmarks spanning mathematics, science, medicine, and code generation, REST improves accuracy by up to 7.5 percentage points and convergence on a final answer by 30% over CE-only training, with no architectural changes or added inference parameters. This work addresses a core weakness in latent reasoning systems, where models recur on hidden states or pass them between agents but are only supervised on the final decoded answer, leaving the thought itself unconstrained. By making latent thoughts more causally meaningful and interpretable, REST could improve how LLMs reason in continuous space and make latent communication between agents easier to interpret, with implications for single-agent and multi-agent reasoning research. REST is instantiated in both latent single-agent and multi-agent systems without architectural changes or added parameters at inference, and is evaluated with the same training data, compute, and latent budget as the CE-only baseline. The paper reports that REST thoughts encode more of what is required to achieve the correct answer and that decoding them better recovers the intended output of the agent.

rss · ArXiv CS.AI · Sep 30, 04:00

**Background**: Latent recursive LLM systems let a model reason in continuous space by recurring on its own hidden states or by passing those states between agents, rather than decoding intermediate text. Standard training supervises only the cross-entropy of the final decoded answer, which does not constrain the internal thought representation. Cross-entropy loss measures the negative log-probability the model assigns to the correct next token, so it says nothing about whether the hidden thought is causally useful, minimal, separable, or stable. REST adds differentiable losses enforcing those four properties on top of cross-entropy.

## [CoWindow and MassAlloc Attention Cut Long-Context Redundant Compute](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wt1gbk/cowindow_and_massalloc_attention_collective/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The authors of two new papers introduce CoWindow Attention (CoWA), which distributes distant context across KV heads using complementary windows while sharing local and prefix-sink windows, and MassAlloc Attention (MALA), which uses attention's own softmax statistics to skip low-contribution post-score computation. At 128K tokens on 8 H100 GPUs with TP=8, CoWA achieves 7.4x/8.6x/3.0x operator speedups for forward/backward/decode versus FullAttn, while MALA achieves 2.2x/3.0x/1.6x, with training FLOPs reduced by 28.5% and 23.1% respectively at 14B scale with 32K context. Long-context models are bottlenecked by the quadratic cost of attention, and these two methods attack redundant computation from complementary angles—spatial coverage and adaptive allocation—potentially making 128K-token training and inference substantially cheaper. If the reported operator-level gains translate to end-to-end savings, they could lower the cost of training and serving long-context LLMs, though the authors caution that neither method establishes universal lossless equivalence to dense attention. CoWA's pattern is position-defined and requires no learned router or indexer, and each head attends sparsely while the union of visible positions covers the full causal history; MALA still pays for full causal QK scoring and only skips low-contribution post-score work using a common tolerance across training and inference. The reported speedups are for attention operators only, not end-to-end model speedups, and evaluations span 0.6B to 14B models plus separate continued-training experiments at 32B.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/BitExternal4608 · Sep 29, 05:16

**Background**: In transformer attention, causal masking ensures each token only attends to previous tokens, and multi-head attention gives each head its own query, key, and value projections. Grouped-query attention reduces the number of KV heads to shrink the KV cache, while FlashAttention-style online softmax tracks running statistics to compute attention efficiently in tiles. These papers build on that landscape by exploiting two sources of redundancy: distant context that is repeatedly covered across heads, and attention weights whose softmax contributions are too small to matter.
